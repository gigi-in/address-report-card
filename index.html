How this is calculated

Where does the data come from?

CTA L stops, Metra stations, Chicago Public Library branches, Chicago Park District parks (and their indoor facilities) and a list of grocery stores are built into this page. The address lookup (OpenStreetMap's Nominatim) and walking routes (OpenStreetMap's Valhalla) need an internet connection.

How are walking times measured?

The page finds the three closest places of each kind in a straight line, asks for a real walking route to each, and uses the shortest. If the route service can't be reached, it estimates from straight-line distance (x1.3 for streets, at 4.8 km/h) and marks the result "(estimated)". For parks, the walk is to the nearest point on the park's boundary.

What do Good, Fair and Bad mean?

Good means within the limit shown on the card. Fair means over the limit but within the Fair multiplier (1.5 times by default). Bad means beyond that. Change any limit or the multiplier under Limits.

How is the overall rating decided?

Overall is Bad if any measure is Bad, Good if every measure is Good, and Fair otherwise. A measure that couldn't be checked is shown as INFO and left out.

How is "downtown by train" estimated?

It adds the walk to the nearest station to a ride estimate from that station to Clark/Lake in the Loop. The ride is a distance-based estimate (distance x1.2, at about 30 km/h for the L and 45 km/h for Metra), not a schedule. It leaves out waiting and transfers, so tools like Google Maps will usually show a longer time.

What counts as an indoor rec space?

Chicago Park District parks that have indoor recreation: a community center, gymnasium, gymnastics room, indoor pool, fitness center, boxing gym, climbing wall or indoor handball court. Private venues, such as climbing gyms and pickleball facilities, can be added to the list but are not included yet. Walks are measured to the nearest point on the park boundary, not to the building door.

How is crime measured?

Reported incidents in three groups (violent, property and other) are totaled for map cells within half a mile of the address in the latest full calendar year, then ranked against the rest of the city. A lower percentile is better. The rating uses the percentile (Good at 50 or below, Fair up to the Fair multiplier). Violent covers homicide, sexual assault, robbery, battery, assault and kidnapping. Property covers theft, burglary, vehicle theft, criminal damage and arson. Other covers weapons violations, trespass, stalking, intimidation and similar. Drug, vice and fraud reports are left out because they reflect enforcement or are not tied to where a person lives. Counts are not per resident, incident locations are rounded to the block for privacy, and reports reflect what is reported as well as what happens. It describes an area, not your personal risk. Until the crime data is added, these cards show INFO.

What about the surrounding neighborhoods?

A second card looks at the ring between half a mile and one mile from the address and ranks it against the same ring around every other spot in the city. If most of the nearby incidents are on one side (30% or more), it says which direction. Areas outside Chicago are not in the data, so addresses near the city limits or the lake can look better than they are, because part of the ring falls outside the data.

How is the crime trend calculated?

The trend compares the total number of incidents within half a mile in the latest full year with the first year in the data, next to the same change for the whole city. If the area changed less than the city, it improved relative to the city even when the raw number went up. With fewer than 20 incidents in either year the page says the numbers are too small to judge, because small counts swing a lot from year to year. Partial years are not used.

What does crime by time of day show?

It shows what share of nearby incidents happen in each part of the day (early morning is midnight to 6 am, morning is 6 am to noon, afternoon is noon to 6 pm, and evening is 6 pm to midnight) with all years combined, and how that compares with the city overall. It is for information only and does not affect the rating.

What are the limitations?

The lists are snapshots and can go out of date. The grocery list may be incomplete. Park distances are approximate because park outlines are simplified to points about 15 meters apart. The nearest station on foot isn't always the fastest way downtown.
